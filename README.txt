Tiny World V10.1 — AGES, LIVING HISTORY & CIVILIZATION SCALE

V10.1 is a massive civilization expansion built on the optimized V10.0.2 base.

MAJOR FEATURES

1. STONE AGE
- Every independent civilization begins in the Stone Age.
- Stone Age homes render as primitive hide/timber shelters.
- Citizens use a more primitive earth-tone clothing palette.
- History begins as oral tradition.

2. BRONZE AGE
- Requires population, knowledge and stoneworking progress.
- Forge development begins.
- Villages become more organized and historical records improve.

3. IRON AGE
- Requires mining and real iron reserves.
- Iron becomes part of the progression gate rather than a cosmetic resource.

4. MEDIEVAL AGE
- Larger settlements, roads and food infrastructure unlock a mature kingdom era.
- Libraries and guild halls begin appearing.
- History becomes true written chronicle.

5. INDUSTRIAL AGE
- Factories and power plants appear.
- Industrial infrastructure becomes a requirement for further advancement.

6. TECHNOLOGY AGE
- Research labs and hospitals appear.
- High education and research infrastructure accelerate knowledge.

7. SPACE AGE
- Advanced civilizations can construct a spaceport.
- Space programs consume real food, iron and gold.
- A completed program launches a visible rocket.
- The first successful program creates a persistent world milestone:
  FIRST HUMAN LANDING ON THE MOON.

8. INDEPENDENT CIVILIZATION PROGRESSION
- Every settlement tracks its own age, knowledge, age-start day and age history.
- Daughter settlements can begin behind their parent civilization.
- Different societies can occupy different ages simultaneously.

9. KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM
- Population, education, skilled workers, workshops, libraries, trade and labs contribute.
- Advancement is not based only on waiting for time to pass.
- Each age has infrastructure/resource requirements.

10. LIVING HISTORY
- Major world events generate civilization-specific historical accounts.
- The same event can be remembered differently by different societies.
- Religions, cultural values and whether a civilization participated in an event affect interpretation.
- Records evolve from oral tradition → carved record → written chronicle → printed archive → digital/orbital archive.
- Later ages preserve history with greater reliability.

11. DEEPER RELIGION
- Religions now maintain multiple teachings.
- Actual citizens can become Prophets, High Priests and Priests.
- Religious leadership can continue after the original prophet.
- New teachings can emerge from religion + culture over time.

12. RELIGIOUS POLICY
- Settlements can become Tolerant, Traditional, Missionary, Theocratic or Secular.
- Policy emerges from leaders and belief instead of being assigned manually.

13. PILGRIMAGES
- Devout named citizens can physically travel to remembered holy sites.
- Reaching a holy site strengthens belief and creates a personal memory.
- Pilgrims then travel home.

14. CULTURAL CONTINUITY
- Migrants and refugees retain cultural attachment.
- First generations resist instant assimilation.
- Younger/lower-attachment citizens can gradually adopt the host culture.
- Cultural heroes gain historical titles.

15. CULTURAL VISUAL IDENTITY
- Mature cultures influence citizen clothing.
- Buildings receive small culture-colored banners/accents.

16. SMALLER WORLD OBJECTS
- People reduced to roughly 72% of prior visual size.
- Creatures reduced to roughly 52%.
- Buildings reduced to roughly 62%.
- Farms reduced to roughly 58%.
- Trees reduced to roughly 76%.
- UI and camera scale remain unchanged.

17. DENSER SETTLEMENTS
- Building spacing is significantly tighter.
- Housing cost/spacing was adjusted to allow larger visible towns on the same island.
- More homes, markets, civic structures and advanced infrastructure can coexist.

18. FULL BUILDING INSPECTION
Tap a building in Inspect mode to see:
- Generated building name
- Type
- Settlement
- Age in which it was built
- Construction/completion day
- Actual builder when known
- Condition
- Upgrade level
- Residents for homes
- Associated workers for workplaces
- Farm growth/harvest information
- Space-program status for spaceports

19. BUILDING HISTORY
Every building owns a persistent historical log.
Examples include:
- Construction started
- Resources committed
- Actual citizen who completed it
- Civilization entering a new age
- Repairs
- Fire/heat damage
- Lunar missions launched from a spaceport

20. BUILDING CONDITION
- Completed structures slowly age.
- Fires/extreme heat can damage structures.
- Builders can spend wood to repair worn buildings.
- Repairs and damage become part of that building's history.

21. ADVANCED BUILDINGS
V10.1 adds:
- Forge
- Library
- Guild Hall
- Factory
- Power Plant
- Research Lab
- Hospital
- Spaceport

22. ROCKET VISUAL
- Lunar launches produce an actual ascending rocket/flame/smoke event on the world.

23. AGES SCREEN
World → Ages shows:
- Every settlement's current age
- Knowledge
- Progress to next age
- Historical record medium
- Age start date
- Space-program state
- Global Moon landing milestone

24. CHRONICLES SCREEN
World → Chronicles shows the selected civilization's own account of world history,
including record reliability and the original world event it was interpreting.

PERFORMANCE
V10.1 preserves the V10.0.1 + V10.0.2 optimization architecture:
- Cached AI resource targets
- Indexed people/building/settlement data
- Staggered simulation systems
- Adaptive world rendering
- Visible entity culling
- Cached tree sprites
- Fast resource brushes
- No unnecessary terrain rebuild for Trees/Food/Stone/Iron/Gold/etc.
